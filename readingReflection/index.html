The Design of Everyday Things - Chapter 1

Affordances & Signifiers

The real affordances of my micro project are people who have a computer and who are hearing people. My micro project does not have the affordance for someone who is deaf or hard of hearing. Also, someone who is not using a computer and rather viewing my micro project on a phone; the instructions are not visible. Meaning my micro project does not have the affordance for someone using a phone or handheld device.

Feedback

The system provides feedback once the first screen when the system displays instructions and a button. The second feedback that is displayed is when the button is pressed and the flower starts breathing. One moment that I think my systems fails to communicate the state clearly is when the flower is breathing and the audio is playing, nowhere on my project does it say that there will be audio.

Mapping

I believe that the relationship between input and outcome is intuitive because when user sees that a button is displayed and pressed; the system shows the flower breathing. I do not think there is anything to misinterpret, when the reading talks about J.J. Gibson work when it says, “information pickup”. The user sees the button, and it is signifying that when the button is pressed an affordance is there to access the next screen.

Constraints

I do believe that there are constraints that exist on my project, and it is the audio aspect. The constraints are invisible to the viewer, when the button is pressed, and the flower starts to breathe and audio plays. There are no signifiers that tell the viewer there is an audio affordance, and I believe that is my biggest constraint.

The Biggest Design Weakness

Again, the biggest weakness of my design is that it fails to indicate to users that there will be audio. People who are hard of hearing, deaf, or even just have their sound off will not know that there is audio playing because there are no perceived signifiers. To improve accessibility, I could incorporate visual cues such as icons, captions, or subtle animations that clearly communicate the presence of sound. Adding user controls like volume indicators or prompts would also help ensure that all users, regardless of ability or context, can understand and engage with the experience.
